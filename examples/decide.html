Decision memo · Northwind platform team

Move the platform team to a one-week on-call rotation in January

Response to pages slows by more than half in the second week of a two-week shift. Halving the shift keeps everyone in the fast first week, costs no new hire, and can be reversed at any handover.

Version 222 Oct 2026For approval by the platform lead5 min read
Bottom line

Approve a one-week on-call shift for the six platform engineers, starting Monday 5 January, with a review on 30 March.

  • Why now: median time to acknowledge a page rises from 4.3 minutes in week one to 7.5 in week two, and two of six engineers have asked to leave the rotation.
  • Cost: no new hire. Each engineer is on call the same number of days a year, in shorter stretches, with twice as many handovers (52 a year instead of 26).
  • Stop if: any handover drops an open incident, or week-one response is no faster by the March review.

Six engineers share a two-week shift that pages about four times a night

Each engineer holds the pager for fourteen days, then hands over to the next. Pages arrive mostly at night, when batch jobs run. The figures come from the paging system export for July to September.

The rotation is now losing people. In the September survey, which all six answered, two engineers asked to come off the rotation and named the second week as the reason. If they leave it, the remaining four each hold the pager one week in four instead of one in six, which would push more people toward the same request. Hiring a seventh engineer takes about four months, based on the last two platform hires.

Response time tracks days on shift, so a shorter shift is faster

The paging export shows how long each page waited before someone acknowledged it. Grouped by the day of the shift, the wait is flat through week one and climbs every day of week two. A one-week shift removes the second week without changing how many days each person covers in a year.

Hiring helps the load but not the second week, and it lands after the people asking to leave have decided. Doing nothing keeps response near five and a half minutes only while all six stay on the rotation.

The switch takes three steps and ends with a review in March

  1. Publish the new schedule (platform lead, by 1 December)One-week shifts from Monday 5 January, handover Mondays at 10:00, each engineer's weeks named a quarter ahead.
  2. Write the handover checklist (on-call owner, by 15 December)Open incidents, silenced alerts, risky changes planned for the week. The checklist is the handover; nothing is passed on by memory.
  3. Review the first twelve weeks (platform lead, 30 March)Compare response by day of shift with FIG-1, count dropped handovers, and ask the team again. Keep, adjust or revert using the stop conditions.

Two failures would send us back to two weeks

Stop conditionsRevert at the next handover if any handover drops an open incident, or if week-one median response is not below 5 minutes by the March review.

The written checklist guards against context lost at the extra handovers. The March review also tests the other explanation, harder incidents late in the month, by comparing day of shift with day of month.
