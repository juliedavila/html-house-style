# HTML house style

Build every standalone HTML page with this skill. Generated pages tend to share one look (gradient heroes, emoji cards, tiny diagrams), and readers of serious documents find that look untrustworthy. This kit replaces it.

## Order of precedence

1. The reader's explicit request.
2. The host project's existing design system or framework. Inside a codebase that already has its own CSS and components, apply the writing and structure rules here and leave its styling alone unless asked.
3. This guide.

A page generated by code in the repository is changed at its generator, never in the emitted HTML. Inside a host that has its own tests and specs, those replace `check.mjs` and this kit's structure rules; apply only the writing rules, and only where the host has none. A multi-page or multi-view app with routes is out of scope, even when it ships as one generated file.

If the reader asks for a different palette, font or layout, do it and keep the rest of the rules. If they say a page is only for a desktop tool, the phone layout can be rough, but it must not break.

## Files in this skill

- `PATTERNS.md`: the six page patterns (Decide, Understand, Look up, Monitor, Do, Review). Each one sets the opening, the order of sections, the navigation, what is stored and how the page prints. Read section 1 and the pattern you choose every time.
- `examples/`: one short working page per pattern, plus a contents page. Open the one for your pattern and copy its skeleton.
- `STYLE-GUIDE.md`: the full guide, with the reason behind each rule. Read sections 1 to 3 and 11 the first time you use this skill in a session, then the section for what you are building (diagrams and charts: 7; tools and dashboards: 13).
- `base.css`: the stylesheet. Inline the whole file into a `<style>` tag in every page.
- `template.html`: a decision brief (the Decide pattern), followed by a component gallery that shows every component in `base.css` once. Its head and closing scripts are the ones every page copies.
- `CHECKLIST.md`: run it before you hand anything over.
- `tools/`: `bars.mjs` (a bar chart figure from data), `table.mjs` (a table, or a whole `section.dataset`, from records) and `selftest.mjs`. Each runs as a module or a command.
- `check.mjs`: a static checker with no dependencies. `node check.mjs page.html` (or `bun`, or `deno run --allow-read`). It needs no browser.

## How to build a page

1. **Name the reader's job** (PATTERNS.md section 1). Ask what the reader will do in the minute after they open the page, and pick Decide, Understand, Look up, Monitor, Do or Review. For a mixed request, build the skeleton of the job the reader does first and borrow at most one section from one other pattern. Declare it: `<body class="doc" data-pattern="decide">`. Never invent a seventh pattern.
2. **Set the density and palette from the pattern.** Its entry in PATTERNS.md gives both. In general, use `body class="tool"` when the reader mainly scans, filters or operates the page and reads fewer than about 300 words of prose; otherwise `body class="doc"`. Palette: `paper` for anything read at length, `slate` for the most conservative audience, `graphite` for dense tools.
3. **Write the opening the pattern names, first.** Decide and Review pages open with `.bluf`: one answer sentence, then a short list (for Decide: the ask, why now, the cost, the stop conditions; for Review: the counts by severity and what to fix first). Understand, Look up and Do pages open with one `.purpose` sentence saying what the page is for; a Do page adds its preconditions. A Monitor page puts one computed status sentence in `.bluf`, with no list, and an "as of" time before the first `h2`. Then write every argument `h2` as a claim a reader could disagree with. Navigation sections (Glossary, Sources, Method, Appendix, Detail) keep plain labels, go last, and carry `class="annex"`.
4. **Copy the example page for your pattern** (`examples/`), or `template.html` for a Decide page. Replace the `<link rel="stylesheet" href="base.css">` (or `../base.css`) with the full contents of `base.css` inside `<style>`. From the template, delete the component gallery. Keep the masthead, the opening, the head script and the closing script block; the closing script holds the filter, step progress and print blocks the patterns use. Keep the contents rail when the page has four or more `h2`s or about 1,200 words or more (Look up, Monitor and Do pages navigate by their index, filters or progress line instead), and write its list into the HTML yourself, with the `.bluf` before `nav.toc`. Delete the palette toolbar unless the page is a tool the reader will live in.
5. **Fill the body** in the order the pattern's skeleton gives, with the components in STYLE-GUIDE section 6. Use tokens for every colour. Wrap every table in `.table-wrap` and put its source in a `.table-note` after the wrapper. Put every figure in `figure`, with the Enlarge button first, then `.figure-frame`, then a caption whose first sentence is a `<strong>` takeaway.
6. **Faster with data.** When the page has real data, generate its charts and tables with `tools/bars.mjs` and `tools/table.mjs` instead of placing coordinates by hand; for more than about 25 records, generate a `section.dataset` (a chart that filters a sortable table). Check each generated piece with `node check.mjs --fragment piece.html` before you splice it in. With no shell, read the module and follow the same arithmetic by hand, and write it in the handover.
7. **Draw diagrams and charts as inline SVG** with the `.d-*` and `.c-*` classes so they follow light and dark. Give every figure SVG `style="--fig-min: Wpx"`, where W is its viewBox width; it then never draws below 1:1, so the 13 and 12 unit label classes show at 13px and 12px. Compute chart coordinates from the data and write the scale in a comment. Use `--cat-1` to `--cat-6` through `.d-cat-N`, `.c-cat-N` and `.key.cat-N` for categories, never a colour in an SVG attribute. Keep any Mermaid source in `<script type="text/plain" class="diagram-source">`, never in an HTML comment (its `-->` arrows end the comment early). Never load a library.
8. **Write plainly.** Define each specialist term on first use with `.term` and `data-def`, and list every term in the glossary. Spell out acronyms on first use. Every measured or external figure names its source and date; your own estimates say they are estimates. No internal codes as names. No em dashes.
9. **Verify, at the highest tier you have.** When the page replaces or restyles an existing one, run `node check.mjs new.html --was old.html` (or, with no shell, list the old page's interactions by reading it) and name in the handover every interaction kept and every one dropped.
   - **Tier 1, a browser is available:** run `check.mjs` if you have a shell, then everything in CHECKLIST "Verified by looking".
   - **Tier 2, no browser:** run `check.mjs` if you have a shell. With no shell, do the same checks by reading the file (CHECKLIST "Verified by reading"), and write the label-size arithmetic for each figure into your handover. Fix what fails. Hand over with: "Not rendered. Checked by reading: [list]. Please look at: [the two or three things only a render can confirm, such as the figure on a phone, dark mode, print]."
   - Never write "I looked at it" or "it renders correctly" at Tier 2.

## Non-negotiables

- One self-contained `.html` file. No CDNs, frameworks, web fonts, charting libraries or network requests. Vanilla JavaScript only, and the page reads correctly with it off.
- One reader's job per page, declared with `data-pattern`, and the pattern's opening and skeleton.
- Answer first; claim headings for the argument; sequential plans as numbered steps.
- A view someone might share lives in the URL hash; personal progress lives in `localStorage` for that page; nothing else is stored.
- Serif for reading text, system sans for interface, monospace for data. Restrained colour: one accent, a second used rarely, status always a shape plus a word.
- Space and hairlines before boxes. Cards only for real groups, never touching.
- Every figure label is at least 12px at its displayed size, and every figure can be enlarged.
- The page never scrolls sideways on a phone.
- You say what you checked and how. You never claim to have looked at a page you did not render.
