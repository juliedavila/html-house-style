Seventeen of 140 services need action within a month

Every service presents a Transport Layer Security (TLS) certificate that proves who it is to its callers. When one expires, callers that check it refuse to connect, and the service is down for them even though it is running. The scan on 20 October read the certificate of every service endpoint. 123 are valid for more than 30 days; the rest are below.

Expiry comes from manual renewal and late alerts

Findings run from most to least severe.

1. Nine certificates have already expired

HighFix this week
Where
mobile-web, mobile-worker, platform-admin-4, search-web-3, payments-web, platform-notify-4, accounts-reports, platform-cache, platform-search
Evidence
The scan read an expiry date before 22 October on each. The oldest expired 28 days ago.
Impact
Any caller that verifies the certificate fails to connect. Callers that skip verification still connect, which is why some of these went unnoticed.
Fix
Renew each one, then confirm with a second scan of those endpoints.

2. Two teams renew most certificates by hand

MediumFix before December
Where
Data (9 of 19 services) and Mobile (11 of 24)
Evidence
20 of the 30 manual renewals belong to these two teams. Pick Data or Mobile in the dataset chart, then Manual, to see them.
Impact
A renewal that depends on a person's calendar fails when that person is away.
Fix
Move both teams to the automatic renewal the other four teams use.

3. The only alert fires after a certificate expires

LowFix next quarter
Where
Monitoring rules for all services
Evidence
The certificate alert triggers on a failed handshake, which happens only once the certificate has expired.
Impact
Owners learn of an expiry from their callers.
Fix
Add a warning at 30 days and a page at 7 days.

The scan checked every endpoint once, from inside the network

Each service endpoint in the service catalogue was contacted once on 20 October 2026, from a host inside the network, and its certificate's expiry date, issuer and renewal setting were recorded. Days left are counted from 22 October. Services were matched to teams by the catalogue's owner field.

Three things this audit did not check

  • Certificates used between services inside a cluster, which the platform rotates on its own schedule.
  • Certificates on third-party services we call.
  • Whether each caller verifies certificates at all.
