The checkout is not ready to launch: three findings stop customers paying

A review of the three checkout steps against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, with a keyboard and two screen readers.

Version 1Reviewed 20 and 21 Oct 20268 findings5 min read
Verdict

Do not launch the new checkout until the three high-severity findings are fixed; all three are small changes.

  • Counts: 3 high, 3 medium and 2 low, out of 8 findings.
  • Fix first: the Place order button, which no keyboard can reach (finding 1). It is a one-line change and unblocks every keyboard user.
  • Then: retest the three steps, about half a day, before the launch date of 3 November.

1. The Place order button cannot be reached with a keyboard

HighFix before launch
Where
Step 3, Review and pay: the Place order button
Evidence
The button is a div with a click handler. Pressing Tab moves from the promo code field straight to the footer links.
Impact
Anyone who uses a keyboard or a switch device cannot place any order.
Fix
Make it a button type="submit". No visual change.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 2.1.1 Keyboard

2. The card number field has no label a screen reader can announce

HighFix before launch
Where
Step 2, Payment: card number
Evidence
The field has only placeholder text. Both screen readers tested announce it as "edit text", and the placeholder disappears once typing starts.
Impact
Screen reader users cannot tell which field takes the card number.
Fix
Add a visible label tied to the field with for.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 1.3.1 Info and Relationships; 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value

3. Payment errors are shown only in red and are never announced

HighFix before launch
Where
Step 3, Review and pay: the declined-card message
Evidence
A declined card turns the card field border red and shows red text at the top of the page. Focus stays on the button, and neither screen reader says anything.
Impact
Blind customers do not learn that payment failed; some colour-blind customers cannot tell which field is wrong.
Fix
Put the error text beside its field, link it with aria-describedby, and move focus to an error summary at the top.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 1.4.1 Use of Color; 3.3.1 Error Identification; 4.1.3 Status Messages

4. Focus outlines are removed across the whole checkout

MediumFix before launch
Where
All three steps
Evidence
The checkout stylesheet sets outline: none on every element, and nothing replaces it.
Impact
Keyboard users cannot see where they are on the page.
Fix
Delete the rule and add a visible focus style that meets 3 to 1 contrast.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 2.4.7 Focus Visible

5. Hint text under the fields fails contrast at 2.8 to 1

MediumFix next sprint
Where
Steps 1 and 2: the hint lines under each field
Evidence
Grey #9A9A9A on white measures 2.8 to 1 with a contrast checker. The target for text this size is 4.5 to 1.
Impact
Customers with low vision, or on a phone outdoors, cannot read the format hints.
Fix
Darken the hint colour to at least #6B6B6B (5.3 to 1).
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)

6. The session ends after ten minutes with no warning

MediumFix next sprint
Where
All three steps
Evidence
Leaving the payment step open for ten minutes returns to an empty cart with no message.
Impact
Customers who need more time, including many who use assistive technology, lose their order and start again.
Fix
Warn at eight minutes and let the customer extend the session with one button.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable

7. The order summary table has no header cells

LowFix next sprint
Where
Step 3, Review and pay: order summary
Evidence
Item, quantity and price are plain cells. A screen reader reads the numbers with no column names.
Impact
Screen reader users hear a list of numbers they have to work out.
Fix
Use th for the column headers.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 1.3.1 Info and Relationships

8. Address fields do not declare their purpose for autofill

LowAccepted
Where
Step 1, Delivery: address fields
Evidence
The fields have no autocomplete values, so browsers cannot fill them in.
Impact
Customers with motor or memory impairments type the whole address by hand.
Fix
Accepted for now: the new address form ships in December with these values set.
Standard
WCAG 2.2, 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose

The review covered three steps on two browsers

The three checkout steps (delivery, payment, review and pay) on the staging site, build of 19 October. Each step was completed with a keyboard only, with two screen readers on a laptop and a phone, and at 200% zoom. Contrast was measured with a contrast checker; markup was read in the browser's developer tools. Each finding was reproduced twice before it was written down.

Four things this review did not check

  • The payment provider's card form inside its frame, which the provider tests.
  • Voice control software.
  • Pages before the basket, such as search and product pages.
  • The emails sent after an order.
